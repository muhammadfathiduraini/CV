The Responsibility to Protect and the Use of Force

An academic examination of the relationship between the Responsibility to Protect framework, state sovereignty and the international legal regulation of the use of force.

Author Muhammad Fathi Duraini bin Sahaimi
Institution Universiti Sultan Zainal Abidin (UniSZA)
Field International Relations · International Law

Abstract

This research examines the relationship between the Responsibility to Protect (R2P) and the international legal framework governing the use of force. It considers the development of R2P as a political and normative framework and examines its relationship with the principles of sovereignty, non-intervention and the prohibition of the use of force.

The research further considers the role of the United Nations Security Council and the limits imposed by existing international law. The analysis distinguishes between the political commitments associated with R2P and the legally established rules governing the use of force.

1. Introduction

The protection of populations from genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing and crimes against humanity has become an important issue within contemporary international relations.

The Responsibility to Protect emerged from debates concerning the relationship between state sovereignty and international responsibility for the protection of populations facing mass atrocity crimes.

The central question examined in this research is how R2P interacts with the existing international legal framework concerning the use of force.

2. Conceptual and Legal Framework

2.1 Sovereignty and Non-Intervention

State sovereignty remains a foundational principle of the international legal order. The principle of sovereign equality is reflected in the Charter of the United Nations and forms part of the broader structure governing relations between states.

2.2 Prohibition of the Use of Force

Article 2(4) of the United Nations Charter establishes a general prohibition on the threat or use of force against the territorial integrity or political independence of states.

2.3 Responsibility to Protect

The Responsibility to Protect developed through international political and diplomatic processes, particularly following the 2005 World Summit.

3. Analysis

The relationship between R2P and the use of force requires careful distinction between political responsibility and legal authorisation.

R2P does not independently create a general legal exception to the prohibition on the use of force. Where coercive military action is contemplated, questions concerning the authority of the Security Council and the applicable rules of the UN Charter remain central.

This distinction is important because the existence of a humanitarian crisis does not, by itself, automatically determine the legality of military intervention.

4. Conclusion

R2P represents an important development in international discourse concerning the protection of populations from mass atrocity crimes. However, its relationship with the use of force must be understood within the existing international legal framework.

The distinction between normative responsibility, political decision-making and legal authorisation remains essential when analysing the contemporary application of R2P.
