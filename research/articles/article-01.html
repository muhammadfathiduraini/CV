The Responsibility to Protect and the Use of Force

Examining the relationship between the Responsibility to Protect framework, state sovereignty, and the use of force in international relations.

AUTHOR Muhammad Fathi Duraini bin Sahaimi
INSTITUTION Universiti Sultan Zainal Abidin
FIELD International Relations · International Law
STATUS Undergraduate Academic Research
Contents Abstract Introduction Conceptual Framework Legal Framework Analysis Conclusion References
01

Abstract

The Responsibility to Protect, commonly referred to as R2P, emerged from international debates concerning the protection of populations from genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing, and crimes against humanity. Its development has generated important questions concerning sovereignty, international responsibility, collective action, and the circumstances in which force may be considered.

This article examines the relationship between the Responsibility to Protect and the use of force through an international relations and international legal perspective. It considers the development of R2P, its relationship with state sovereignty, and the central role of the United Nations Security Council in the authorisation of coercive measures.

02

Introduction

The question of whether and when the international community may act to protect populations from mass atrocity crimes remains one of the most difficult issues in contemporary international relations. The traditional principle of sovereignty places states at the centre of the international legal order, while the development of international human rights and humanitarian norms has increasingly emphasised the protection of individuals and communities.

The Responsibility to Protect represents an attempt to reconcile these developments. Rather than treating sovereignty exclusively as control over territory and domestic affairs, R2P places emphasis on the responsibility of states to protect populations within their jurisdiction.

The central question examined in this article is therefore not simply whether R2P permits the use of force, but how R2P interacts with the existing international legal framework governing the use of force.

03

Conceptual Framework

The Responsibility to Protect

R2P developed from concerns that the international community had repeatedly failed to prevent or respond effectively to mass atrocity crimes. The framework places primary responsibility on individual states to protect their populations while recognising a broader international responsibility to assist states and respond collectively where national authorities fail to fulfil that responsibility.

Sovereignty as Responsibility

One of the conceptual developments associated with R2P is the understanding of sovereignty as involving responsibility. Under this approach, sovereign authority is connected with the protection of populations rather than being understood solely as freedom from external interference.

04

Legal Framework

The use of force in international relations is principally governed by the United Nations Charter. Article 2(4) establishes a general prohibition on the threat or use of force against the territorial integrity or political independence of a state, subject to the exceptions recognised within the Charter framework.

The Security Council has a central role under Chapter VII of the Charter. Where the Council determines the existence of a threat to the peace, breach of the peace, or act of aggression, it may adopt measures under the Charter, including measures involving the use of force.

Consequently, the existence of an R2P situation does not by itself establish an independent legal authority to use force. The relationship between R2P and coercive action must be considered alongside the existing rules and institutional mechanisms of the United Nations system.

05

Analysis

R2P and Collective Security

The relationship between R2P and the use of force is closely connected to the collective security system established by the United Nations Charter. R2P can provide a normative framework for understanding international responsibility, while the Charter determines the legal mechanisms through which coercive measures may be undertaken.

This distinction is significant. A normative commitment to protect populations should not automatically be equated with a unilateral legal authority to use force. The institutional process through which international action is authorised remains central to questions of legality and legitimacy.

Sovereignty and International Responsibility

R2P also demonstrates the continuing evolution of sovereignty within international relations. The concept does not simply eliminate sovereignty. Instead, it places greater emphasis on the responsibilities associated with sovereign authority.

Institutional Constraints

The implementation of R2P is nevertheless affected by the political structure of the Security Council. Decision-making depends on the Council's institutional procedures and the political positions of its members. This creates a distinction between the normative recognition of a responsibility to protect and the practical capacity of the international community to act collectively.

06

Conclusion

The Responsibility to Protect represents an important development in international thinking concerning sovereignty and the protection of populations from mass atrocity crimes. Its significance lies in connecting state responsibility with a broader international responsibility to respond to serious violations.

However, R2P should not be understood as creating an unrestricted independent authority to use force. Questions concerning coercive measures remain connected to the international legal framework governing the use of force, particularly the United Nations Charter and the role of the Security Council.

Understanding this relationship requires attention to both the normative development of R2P and the legal and institutional structures through which international action takes place.
