Undergraduate Thesis To Be Developed

A long-form undergraduate research project to be developed through the later stages of the Bachelor of International Relations programme.

Researcher Muhammad Fathi Duraini bin Sahaimi
Institution Universiti Sultan Zainal Abidin
Programme Bachelor of International Relations (Hons.)
Status Forthcoming Undergraduate Research
Research Development
Research Problem Literature & Framework Methodology Analysis & Findings Contribution Current Status
01

Research Problem

The undergraduate thesis will investigate a focused problem within International Relations and develop an analytical argument supported by relevant literature and primary evidence.

The final research question has not yet been fixed. It will be developed through further coursework, literature review, consultation and identification of a sufficiently focused research gap.

02

Literature & Framework

Literature Review

The literature review will establish the existing academic debate surrounding the selected research problem and identify relevant theoretical and conceptual approaches.

Theoretical Framework

The appropriate International Relations theory or analytical framework will be selected after the research question has been sufficiently defined.

Interdisciplinary Perspective

Where relevant, the research may connect International Relations analysis with international law, human rights, humanitarian affairs or international security.

03

Methodology

The research methodology will be determined by the final research question and the type of evidence required.

Possible approaches may include qualitative document analysis, comparative analysis, case study research or structured analysis of primary and secondary sources.

The final methodology will be stated explicitly in the completed thesis rather than assumed at this development stage.

04

Analysis & Findings

This section will contain the substantive findings of the completed undergraduate research.

The analysis will connect the evidence to the research question and theoretical framework while distinguishing documented findings from interpretation.

No findings are presented on this page at this stage because the thesis has not yet been completed.

05

Contribution

The completed thesis will identify its contribution to the relevant academic discussion and explain the significance of its findings within the selected field.

Depending on the final topic, the contribution may relate to an empirical case, a legal or institutional question, a regional perspective or an analytical interpretation of an international issue.

06

Current Status

This thesis page is currently a research-development record. The final title, research question, methodology, findings and references will be added as the undergraduate research project progresses.

The project is therefore presented as forthcoming academic work and not as a completed thesis or published study.
